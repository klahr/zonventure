How it works

Three steps from "we should do something fun" to "team five just finished".

1

Author the adventure

Build a story of puzzle pages with prompts, hints and optional GPS gates. Use the global catalog if you don't want to author your own.

2

Schedule a run

Pick a date and time, name the run, set the team count. Each team gets a magic link you share with the players — no app installs.

3

Watch them play

Live activity feed plus an audience leaderboard for your lobby TV. Webhooks let you pipe events to Slack, Zapier, or your own systems.

Built for the people who run things

Whoever you are, the platform stays out of your way.

Corporate events

Run a treasure hunt as a team-building offsite. Brand the player view with your colours, watch the live board on the lobby TV, post results in your Slack via webhook.

Schools and youth groups

Outdoor learning that gets pupils moving. Optional COPPA-aware age gating. Webhook into your communication tools so parents get a "team finished" notification automatically.

Tourism and city walks

Sell self-guided adventures around your city. One-click "buy team slots" via Stripe; players play whenever it suits them; you pocket the difference.

Things you'll appreciate

The capabilities that make a Saturday-afternoon event run smoothly.

Markdown puzzles

Format prompts with headings, lists, links and images. Hints unlock on a delay so stuck teams get help, fast teams never see them.

GPS-gated answers

Pin a puzzle to a real location with a radius. Players can read the prompt anywhere, but only submit once they're on site.

Live audience board

Public-ish leaderboard URL for your lobby TV. Optional audience map. Map is OFF by default — privacy stays opt-in.

Magic-link players

No app to install, no account to create. Each player taps their link and they're in. Captain links forward easily.

Stripe-powered billing

Buy assignments by the team-slot. Real receipts, real refund flow. Tax handled automatically when Stripe Tax is enabled.

Webhooks for integrators

Seven event kinds (run/team/purchase lifecycle), HMAC-signed POSTs, exponential backoff retry, idempotency headers. Webhook docs.
